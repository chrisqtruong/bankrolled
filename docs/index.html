About

Bankrolled is maintained by Chris Truong. He started it because he wants a fairer, more equitable world, and getting there means watching what large corporations do with their money and power: how they profit from the communities they operate in, and how they buy influence over the people who are supposed to regulate them. Sports betting is the first industry Bankrolled tracks.

What this is

Bankrolled tracks the sports betting industry's money and its effects on people. We take a clear position: this industry profits from losses that fall hardest on young men and on households that can least afford them, and it spends heavily to shape the laws that govern it. Because we take that position, the facts have to be airtight. This page explains how they are gathered and checked.

Confirmed, reported, disputed

ConfirmedBacked by a primary record (a campaign finance or lobbying filing, a court document, a regulator's action, a company filing or statement) or by reputable reporting that cites one. Only confirmed items can enter the Ledger.
ReportedCredible but not yet verifiable: one outlet, unnamed sources, an estimate, or a deal in talks. These appear in the feed, labeled, and never in the Ledger.
DisputedReported by a credible outlet and publicly denied by the subject. We show both sides.

Criminal defendants are described as charged or accused until they plead guilty or are convicted. Lawsuits are allegations. Amounts sought in a lawsuit are claims, not judgments. When sources disagree on a number, we leave the number out and say so.

How a day's update works

01A scheduled research run checks every two hours, from 7:52 a.m. to 11:52 p.m. Eastern. Nobody has to prompt it.
02It checks the primary sources first: FEC filings for the industry's super PACs and corporate PACs, Senate lobbying disclosures, DOJ press releases, CFTC and court dockets, state gaming regulators, and company investor pages.
03It then scans the news: wire services, major papers, and the trade press (Covers, SBC Americas, Legal Sports Report, Sportico, Front Office Sports, InGame), along with targeted news searches on charges, fines, lawsuits, legislation and deals.
04Each candidate item is read at the source, not from a headline. It is checked against what is already published, so nothing is posted twice, and classified as confirmed, reported or disputed.
05New items are written in plain language and added to the feed. Confirmed money is added to the Ledger. The charts update when new data is published (AGA monthly revenue, FEC quarterly reports).
06If nothing meets the bar, nothing is posted. The run still records that the check happened, and the "Updated" date at the top shows the last check.

Where the data lives

The site is a single page that reads one data file, holding feed posts, Ledger rows, chart series and key figures. Each daily run edits that file and republishes the page at the same link. Every entry stores its date, category, status and source links, so the Ledger can be copied out as a spreadsheet at any time with the "Copy table as CSV" button below it.

Sources we watch

  • FEC: Win for America, DraftKings PAC, and the FEC receipts search
  • Senate lobbying disclosures (quarterly) and OpenSecrets
  • AGA revenue tracker (monthly), state regulators, and the NY State Comptroller
  • DraftKings, Flutter/FanDuel and other company investor pages (quarterly)
  • CFTC press releases, EDNY and EDPA press releases, and the Federal Register
  • Research: NBER, NY Fed, Pew, Siena, NCPG

Limits

  • Almost no athlete or celebrity endorsement fee is ever disclosed. The Ledger records each deal and marks the amount "Not disclosed" instead of repeating guesses.
  • The industry's own data, including AGA revenue and AGA-commissioned ad counts, comes from parties with a stake. We use it for the size of the market and note where it comes from.
  • Prediction-market volume is notional (the face value of contracts traded) and is not directly comparable to sportsbook handle.
  • Super PAC data arrives quarterly. Between filings, the political totals do not move.

Archive

The feed shows the last 90 days. Older posts move into a monthly archive at the bottom of the feed and are never deleted, so every post keeps a permanent link. The Ledger and the charts always cover the full history.

Comments

Every post has its own comment thread, stored publicly in this project's GitHub Discussions, so commenting needs a GitHub account. Corrections and new leads with a source are the most useful comments. Comments that harass people or make unsupported accusations are removed.

Corrections

When a fact changes or turns out to be wrong, the entry is corrected in place and the correction is noted in the entry.
